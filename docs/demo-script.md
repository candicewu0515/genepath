# Demo video script (about 4 minutes)

Record the screen at https://candicewu0515.github.io/genepath/ in a desktop-width window. Lines in quotes are narration; lines in brackets are what to do on screen.

## 0:00–0:30 · The problem
[Show the top of the page.]

"Millions of people now have a pharmacogenomic test result. The report says something like 'CYP2C19 star-2 star-2'. Most people can't tell whether that matters for a medicine they already take, or what to do next. GenePath turns that one report into two real decisions: is my medication affected, and is there a clinical trial that fits me."

## 0:30–2:00 · Medication gene card
[Clopidogrel is selected with *2/*2 poor metabolizer. Scroll slowly through the card.]

"Clopidogrel is one of the most prescribed blood thinners. It only works after a liver enzyme, CYP2C19, switches it on. For a poor metabolizer, the card says in plain words that the drug may protect much less well, and that the CPIC guideline recommends an alternative for people with a stent or acute coronary syndrome. It is information for a conversation with a doctor, not an instruction."

[Point at the chart, then the highlighted insight line.]

"This chart uses real aggregate data from the All of Us Research Program, about 535,000 participants. The star-2 variant sits on about 31% of gene copies in East Asian participants and 34% in South Asian participants, more than three times the rate in Middle Eastern participants."

[Click Warfarin. Set VKORC1 to A/A. Point at the East Asian bar.]

"Warfarin shows the gap even more clearly: 87% in East Asian ancestry versus 9% in African ancestry. The same starting dose cannot be right for everyone."

[Scroll to "Questions to take to your next appointment". Click "Print this card", then cancel.]

"The card ends with questions to bring to an appointment, and it prints on one page."

## 2:00–3:20 · Clinical trial navigator
[Click "See recruiting atrial fibrillation trials". The page scrolls and loads live results.]

"The second question: is anyone studying better options for people like me? This is a live query to ClinicalTrials.gov, for atrial fibrillation within 150 miles of Iowa City, filtered to studies open to a 68-year-old and sorted by distance."

[Open the first trial's "Key entry rules, in plain words".]

"Each study shows its three key entry rules in the original wording, with a plain-language note under each one, the nearest sites, and who to contact."

[Tick "Use saved sample data".]

"If the network is down, a saved snapshot keeps everything working."

## 3:20–4:00 · Sources and limits
[Scroll to the footer.]

"Everything here is real, public data: All of Us for frequencies, CPIC guidelines for the recommendations, ClinicalTrials.gov for trials. The page stores nothing and makes no AI calls at run time, so nothing a user enters reaches a model. It is an educational demo: frequencies describe populations, not individuals, and only a study team can confirm eligibility. Next, we want to read a real report file and match trials by genotype. Thank you."
